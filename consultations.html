What actually happens

You're not buying a phone call. You're buying a decision.

Most people book because they've got a high-risk report and no idea whether it means a dehumidifier or a demolition. Half an hour with someone who has opened a thousand walls settles that.

01

You send your scans ahead

Your report history goes with the booking, so nobody spends the first ten minutes catching up.

02

The inspector reads the room with you

Live video. They'll ask you to point the camera at the things a photo missed — the window reveal, the pipe run, the back of the cupboard.

03

You get the honest version

Including “this is fine, dry it out and stop worrying,” which is the outcome more often than people expect.

04

If it needs work, you get a route to it

A scope, a priority order, and an introduction to a trusted local partner who can carry it out.

Before you book

Worth knowing.

No. Scanning, reporting and the whole-house inspection are free and unlimited, forever. Most people never book anything. Consultations exist for the cases where a free answer isn't enough.

The consultation itself is live video with a certified inspector. If the situation needs someone physically in the building, that's the outcome of the consultation — your inspector introduces you to a trusted local partner for the on-site work.

One room and one question: 30 minutes. A whole home, or a problem you've already tried to fix once: 45. A property you're buying, letting or managing, where you need a scoped action plan: 60.

They can give you a written summary you're free to pass on. What that summary is worth is that it comes from a certified professional rather than from you — which is usually the part that changes the conversation.
